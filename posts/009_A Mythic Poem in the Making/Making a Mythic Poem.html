🎭 Unmasking the Heart: A Mythic Poem in the Making

COMM4190 · Personal Dialogue Blog
A collaborative conversation between Inigo & Assistant

Prompt: Write a personal poem that uses mythic characters to explore how we learn to recognize a mistake in love and stop being carried away by the mask.

Note: We’ll move from brainstorm → draft → edits → final, keeping the voice intimate and clear.

Framing the Voice & Stakes

User: I want the poem to feel personal, not like a museum tour of myths. The myths should echo my emotional arc.
Assistant: Perfect. Let’s define three anchors:

  1. Emotional spine (realistic):
    • infatuation → dawning doubt → recognition → release → self-respect
  2. Mythic devices (select a few):
    • Narcissus (self-reflection, illusions)
    • Persephone (descent & return)
    • Ariadne’s thread (finding a way out)
    • Janus (two faces, doors of beginning/end)
    • Sirens (seductive call vs. true voice)
    • Dionysus’ mask (ecstasy, performance, excess)
  3. Imagery/motifs: mirrors, threads, winter/spring, lanterns, tide, masks, echo in a well.

User: Keep Sirens, Janus, Ariadne’s thread, and a subtle nod to Narcissus. The poem should end with a quiet strength.

Micro-Outline (sections & purpose)

Assistant: Here’s a simple scaffold (free verse):

  • I. The Mask Arrives — glamor, noise, a promise that feels like prophecy.
  • II. The Pull — Sirens/sea imagery; your voice gets quieter.
  • III. The Doubt — Janus appears; two faces in one horizon; “Which side did I love?”
  • IV. The Thread — Ariadne’s thread = your boundary & clarity. No labyrinth heroics, just steady choosing.
  • V. The Recognition — small mirror moment (Narcissus, but you look past yourself, not into yourself).
  • VI. The Release — return to shore; you keep the thread, not the mask.
  • VII. The Quiet Strength — a final image: lantern, dawn, tide pulling back.

User: Good. Keep lines lean, lyrical, not ornate. A few strong metaphors, not a flood.

Draft 1 (raw, exploratory)

Assistant: First pass (expect rough edges):

Unmasking

You arrived like a tide that knew my name,
singing—no, arranging the air to sing for you.
I mistook orchestration for omen,
the velvet of your mask for a face that could stay.

Sirens live where language goes to drown.
I heard them in your certainty,
the bright script you handed me like a prophecy,
and I signed where the paper glimmered.

Janus took the doorway of my doubt—
two dawns on one horizon, both persuasive.
Which face did I love?
The answer changed with your weather.

I kept a thread tucked in my sleeve,
a quiet habit of returning to myself.
Ariadne might have called it mercy;
I called it “I will not vanish this time.”

A mirror waited at the bottom of the well:
the water showed a face I nearly didn’t know,
but it was mine—the pulse steadier than surf.
Narcissus glanced up and wandered off;
I let the image move and did not chase it.

The sea returned your borrowed music.
Masks prefer the dark; I walked toward a lantern.
The tide relinquished my name,
and I learned to speak it back, unornamented.

User: I like the bones. Let’s make the voice even more grounded, less “myth dropped in,” more lived.

Edit Goals (what to tighten / deepen)

Assistant: Proposed edits: - Replace big abstractions with tactile details (wet cuff, salt on tongue, paper creasing).
- Trim rhetorical statements; let images carry meaning.
- Keep mythic figures as verbs/adjectives (e.g., “Janus-blue morning”) to feel integrated.
- Aim for a clear pivot from enchantment → boundary.

User: Yes. Add a line where I notice I’m quieter around them. That’s my real tell.

Draft 2 (tighter images, integrated myth)

Assistant: Revised draft:

Unmasking

You came in with the weather—
a practiced brightness, Janus-blue,
asking the room to applaud before the story began.
I mistook the satin curve of your mask
for a promise that wouldn’t rub off.

Your praise foamed like surf.
I learned the taste of salt on my tongue,
how the Sirens don’t shout; they tune the air
until my voice forgets its note.
Around you, I spoke smaller.

Paper prophecies, folded twice—
you passed them like favors and I kept them warm
in the same pocket as my doubts.
The ink bled, then blamed the rain.

I threaded a habit through the day:
a check-in with the person carrying my ribs.
Ariadne would have called it rescue,
but I only counted steps back to a door that knew me.

At the well, the mirror wavered—
not the trap of Narcissus,
more a pulse I could return to without kneeling.
I watched myself breathe, uncoached.

The tide tired of the glitter.
Your mask preferred a darker room.
I kept the thread. I kept my name.
When morning asked for witnesses,
only the lantern answered.

User: That “around you, I spoke smaller” lands. I want one more moment of honest self-critique: I loved the performance because it kept me from asking for anything real.

Final Poem (clean cadence, lived voice) ✨

Unmasking

You came in with the weather—
a practiced brightness, Janus-blue,
asking the room to applaud before the story began.
I mistook the satin curve of your mask
for a promise that wouldn’t rub off.

Your praise foamed like surf.
I learned the taste of salt on my tongue,
how the Sirens don’t shout; they tune the air
until my voice forgets its note.
Around you, I spoke smaller.

I won’t dress it up: the spectacle was shelter.
If you dazzled, I didn’t have to name my needs—
and asking is harder than drowning slowly.

Paper prophecies, folded twice—
you passed them like favors and I kept them warm
in the same pocket as my doubts.
The ink bled, then blamed the rain.

I threaded a habit through the day:
a check-in with the person carrying my ribs.
Ariadne might call it rescue;
I just counted steps back to a door that knew me.

At the well, the mirror wavered—
not a trap, not worship.
Only a pulse I could return to without kneeling.
I watched myself breathe, uncoached.

The tide tired of glitter.
Your mask asked for a darker room.
I kept the thread. I kept my name.
When daylight needed names,
I spoke mine first.

Craft Notes (why these choices work)

Myth as Function, Not Furniture
We kept myths as verbs and textures (“Janus-blue,” “Sirens tune the air,” “Ariadne thread”) so the poem remains personal, not encyclopedic.

  • Mask motif drives the emotional arc: attraction → performance → awareness → autonomy.
  • Honest self-critique (“the spectacle was shelter”) deepens credibility.
  • Bodily anchor (“carrying my ribs”) grounds abstraction.
  • Cadence: short declaratives near the end increase resolve; enjambment keeps momentum.
  • Exit image (“daylight needed names… I spoke mine first”) gives quiet strength without proclaiming victory.

User: This feels like me, but distilled.

Reflection & Next Steps 🧭

Assistant: Two optional extensions (if you ever want to iterate again):

  1. Counter-mask stanza — a brief moment where you practice removing your own mask (what truth would you say plainly?).
  2. Future vow — one concrete behavior you’ll use as “thread” next time (e.g., pausing before mirroring someone’s intensity).

User: For now, I’ll keep the ending light. The vow is implied in speaking my name first.

Takeaway: The poem doesn’t punish feeling—it refuses performance. Myth helps stage the drama, but the decision is human: keep the thread, keep your name.
